The exposé currently lacks a proper introduction and motivation. There is no explanation of why the topic is relevant or worth studying. The first chapter is unfocused and too vague. For example, statements like “research indicated that … can increase task performance” need proper references and more detail.

The motivation to explore gender as a factor in embodied virtual assistants is much too short and underdeveloped. Simply saying that something has not been researched yet is not enough to justify the study. The student needs to explain why this topic matters and how it contributes to existing research.

The only strong point in this section is the formulation of the research questions.

In the structure of the paper, the section currently titled “Approach” is actually the Related Work section. This likely happened due to a LaTeX error and should be corrected.

The related work section itself reads like a list of studies rather than a critical discussion. It does not connect these studies to the student’s own work or explain what lessons were drawn from them. This makes it hard to understand why the student’s study is needed. The writing and citation style also shift noticeably here, suggesting that this part might have been written with an AI tool. The lack of conclusions that tie back to the student’s topic reinforces that impression.

The methodology section is again too vague and lacks important reasoning. For example, the choice of the terms same and opposite gender should be explained. Why not use male and female if those are the intended categories? Also, the current phrasing suggests the study compares homogeneous and heterogeneous gender interactions, but that is not reflected in the research questions. The student should also clarify whether non-binary participants are included or considered.

More details are needed about the study design. For example, how exactly does the system “get the current state of the desert survival problem through a user interface”? The introduction of hints with the structure “move XXX higher/lower” also needs justification. Why is it done this way, and why can the system not explain its recommendations? Similarly, the time limit of 10 minutes per condition must be explained and supported.

Some parts include unnecessary information, such as the need for a room to run the study or that participants must attend in person. The expected participant number should be based on study design, not convenience.

Finally, the section about possible project issues is too shallow. The student mentions potential problems and sets aside a few days to deal with them, but does not specify what these problems might be.
