summary

The given exposé proposes combining Electronic Health Records (EHRs) and Patient Generated Health Data (PGHD) to build a tool that would support doctors in diagnosing patients. Therefore, a literature research on how to combine these two types of data, the training of a LLM, and a study inside a doctor's office will be done. Ethical concerns will be a topic as well.

strengths

  • The motivation part of an exposé deals with a short introduction to the current status quo in a particular field. On that basis, it gets the interest of the reader by proposing a new idea that finds a solution to an existing problem. Your motivation part does that really well. In several paragraphs you describe what the current state is, what is missing, and clearly specify what you are aiming to achieve. The quote in the beginning directly caught my interest.
  • Research questions precisely describe the goals you want to achieve with your scientific work. While being as specific as possible, they leave room for a long answer. You formulated three research questions that fulfil these requirements quite well. They are precise and focus on one problem each. Also, they cannot be answered shortly, which is great. The last one could maybe be split up again, but that is a minor issue.

weaknesses

  • The SOTA section of an exposé gives an overview over the current achievements that were made connected to your field of research. This is important as the reader can be put on the same page as you by gaining knowledge about this field, so that they can understand the necessity of your work better. This part should be very much supported by citations as it heavily relies on the work of others. Your SOTA section is quite short with five sentences. The first two of them make claims without providing a quote that proof them. The last two of them already describe how you want to achieve finding a solution, something that does not belong into the SOTA section. In order to create texts that have a clear structure and are easy to follow, strictly separate the arguments you want to convey. Dedicate one section to every argument and extensively lay it out in that section. This way, a person reading your text understands your chain of thought better.itltations are very important, as they validate the claims you make for the reader. Everyone can do further reading and check if what you say is correct. In the motivation you speak of "current studies", in state of the art there is a sentence beginning with "current research". After these sentences there is no citation. For me as a reader automatically the question evolves what studies you are talking about and I would like to have a look at them. For your development as a scientific researcher, try to add citations at every point in your work where you speak about the work of others, so that for the reader it is understandable what you are talking about. Especially try to avoid speaking generally about "current studies" without directly stating one of them or at least include them as a citation. Rather include "... et. al. found out that ... []".
  • The schedule is a guideline for yourself on how you plan on implementing the ideas you described beforehand. It is as detailed as possible, but leaves sufficient room for errors and unforeseen challenges. The parts of your schedule that are the least predictable seem to be a bit unclear for me, especially weeks 4-14. You plan to do several things at once, recruiting participants while creating the framework for analysing patient data (in one week!) and creating interview plans. Your first sub research question is how to combine EHRs and PGHD, according to the schedule this is going to be solved in a few weeks while also recruiting and developing the framework. In the potential issues section, you describe problems that could arise. These are mainly in the process that is located in weeks 4-14 of the schedule. Still, you plan on doing several things at the same time and plan relatively few time for that. When planning future work of yours, try to keep in mind possible issues more. Plan your time around critical sections and leave enough room for error especially in these sections. If you feel like there will not be enough time, focus on one thing and discard others, before aiming to do more than one thing at once.
  • In the exposé it is important to first identify and describe the problem you are aiming to solve. On the basis of this identification (that happens in the motivation) you then proceed to explain how you want to achieve the solution to this problem. In your motivation, you write that "this work is going to focus on the benefits and drawbacks for the patients". In the approach part, you then speak about a study that is going to be made. This study is to be done with "medical staff". This medical staff is to be interviewed and gets to work with the LLM you aim to build. I like the very clear formulation of the problem you want to solve and who you want to solve it for, I just think that the approach does not exactly match this idea. For future work, try to always keep in mind what goals you want to achieve when thinking about the implementation of your scientific work. Consider changing the study so that focus lays on the patients.
