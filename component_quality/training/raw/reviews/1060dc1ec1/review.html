Summary

The exposé suggests using Large Language Models (LLMs) to analyze students' incorrect or incomplete answers. The goal is to identify the misunderstandings behind those mistakes and use this information to help students learn better. By combining ideas from learning theory, data analysis, and explainable AI, the project aims to turn errors into chances for personalized teaching and support.

Strengths

Focus on Education

The project addresses a real and important problem: understanding why students make mistakes instead of just fixing them. This aligns with modern education goals that emphasize giving feedback and tailoring teaching to individual needs.

Strong Use of Theory

The exposé explains how learning theory, data analysis, and explainable AI (XAI) work together to tackle the problem. These ideas are well connected and provide a strong base for the project.

Balanced Methods

The project will use both numbers (like accuracy and recall) and stories (like interviews with teachers) to gather insights. This mixed approach makes the study more reliable and useful for educators and researchers.

Clear Plan

The timeline is well-organized, with clear tasks and milestones. Including extra time for unexpected delays makes the plan realistic and easy to follow.

Weaknesses

Scope and Feasibility Across Multiple Subjects

The goal is to create a tool that can work well across different subjects like mathematics, science, and language arts, allowing it to identify and address misunderstandings in a wide range of topics (Feed Up). However, the exposé mainly focuses on the idea of being broadly applicable without giving much detail on how the tool will handle the specific needs of different subjects, such as unique terminologies, teaching approaches, or even cultural differences in subject matter (Feed Back). To make this stronger, the exposé could include a clear plan for adapting the tool to different areas. For example, it could outline strategies for customizing the model for individual subjects, mention how domain-specific prompt engineering might be applied, or propose working with subject experts to make sure the tool is accurate and effective for specialized topics (Feed Forward).

Handling of Explainability and Educator Interpretability

The goal is to ensure that the tool explains its decisions in a way that is easy for teachers to understand, helping them see why certain mistakes were flagged and how to address them in their teaching (Feed Up). However, the exposé mainly mentions the importance of explainability but doesn’t go into detail about how this will actually be achieved. For example, it does not explain whether explanations will be visual, provided as examples, or written in a simplified way for non-technical users (Feed Back). To improve this, the exposé could discuss using tools like LIME or SHAP, which break down complex AI outputs into simpler, more understandable components. Additionally, it could describe plans for creating user-friendly interfaces, such as dashboards with clear visuals or written summaries that help educators quickly interpret the information and act on it (Feed Forward).

Reference to Prior Work and State-of-the-Art

The goal is to show how the research builds on or improves existing tools by comparing it to previous work, highlighting what’s new and why it’s valuable (Feed Up). However, the exposé mainly references recent studies but doesn’t fully explore how this project fits into the bigger picture of research. It doesn’t clearly point out what gaps exist in current tools or how this project will fill them (Feed Back). To strengthen this, the exposé could expand its discussion of prior work by mentioning specific examples of LLM tools already being used in education. It could also explain how this tool offers improvements, such as better handling of subject-specific misconceptions, more personalized feedback, or a more user-friendly interface, making its unique contributions clear (Feed Forward).

Evaluation Metrics Beyond Accuracy

The goal is to evaluate the tool not only based on its technical performance, like precision and recall, but also on how well it helps teachers and improves student learning (Feed Up). However, the exposé mainly focuses on accuracy-based metrics without discussing how the tool’s usefulness will be measured in real teaching environments. For example, it doesn’t mention whether it will look at how much time teachers save, how students’ understanding improves, or whether the tool helps reduce repeated mistakes (Feed Back). To improve this, the exposé could propose additional evaluation methods, such as asking teachers to rate how helpful the tool is, measuring how much students improve after using it, or tracking whether the same misconceptions occur less often over time. These practical metrics would make the project’s impact on education more meaningful and easier to measure (Feed Forward).

All the mentioned points are clearly marked in the document to help the creator easily locate where each issue can be found.
