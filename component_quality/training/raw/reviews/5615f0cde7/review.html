This exposé offers an innovative approach to improving STEM education by integrating holographic AI technology, addressing critical challenges such as student engagement, motivation, and teaching spatially complex concepts. It effectively combines AI and holography to create a multimedia learning experience, supported by robust theoretical frameworks like Active Learning Theory, Cognitive Multimedia Learning, and Cognitive Load Theory. These frameworks provide a solid academic foundation, ensuring the study aligns with established pedagogical principles.

The methodology is another strong point, featuring control and experimental groups, qualitative interviews, and quantitative tests. These components ensure a thorough investigation of the research questions. The exposé demonstrates diligent research with 16 referenced sources, but this extensive number could be streamlined by focusing on the most impactful studies to maintain clarity and avoid redundancy.

However, there are areas for refinement. The research questions, particularly RQ1 and RQ2, overlap in scope, as both address aspects of engagement and motivation, and could be combined for greater focus. Additionally, while the challenges of implementing holographic AI assistants are briefly mentioned, they lack depth. Addressing barriers such as cost, accessibility, and scalability would provide a more realistic view of the study’s feasibility. Finally, the choice of the Pepper’s Ghost technique for creating holograms is practical but would benefit from a brief comparison with alternative methods to justify its selection.

Overall, the exposé is innovative and well-structured, with the potential to significantly impact STEM education. By streamlining research questions, elaborating on challenges, and refining technical justifications, the exposé could further strengthen its contribution to the field.
