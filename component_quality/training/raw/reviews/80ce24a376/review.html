The following is a description of the proposed project as I understood it.

The project explores the deployment of holographic AI in education at lower education levels like elementary schools. Thereby it aims to work out the need for holographic AI assistants by identifying benefits and challenges the holograms face in education and the possible acceptance of this technology in education. Additionally specific models will be examined and whether they support key features and if there are important features missing, an own model will be proposed. The methods combine quantitative methods in form of a survey asking for the benefits and challenges and key features at school and literature work examining the models and possibly some technical work when proposing an own model.

The exposé has several strengths.

Firstly in the teaser and later in the State-of-the-art (SOTA) part the gap in research and the position of your own work is clearly emphasized and understandable. This makes it easy for the reader to know why your work is relevant and what you want to research.

Similarly your research questions aid with that as well. While you have many research (sub-) questions, they are all related to each other and specific enough while still being open enough so that you can get good results in your work and are not too restricted by the research questions. As stated already, this also helps the reader position your work and imagine what you want to achieve with it.

In the methodology part of the approach you clearly explain your methodology for answering the first research question. Specifically you mention limitations and problems that could arise and mostly how you would deal with them and also explain your target population and type of method used. This enables the reader to estimate the effort of that part of the project and shows that you thought about the process and are prepared for problems that can arise.

However there are also a few weak points that could be improved a little bit.

The SOTA part aims to explain the SOTA to the reader and then the gap that is left until now. In a good SOTA part the reader has a good idea of how the technology is used and what methods are being used. While you already clearly mention the gap and also what researchers are currently focusing on, the SOTA explanation is a bit short as it does not really explain how the holograms are used in classrooms as of now. You could further elaborate how the countries that have started using holographic AI in classrooms use them to help the students/teachers. This would help the reader understand the technology and its already existing use cases. You could revisit your literature and try to write a small paragraph about the use cases of the holographic AI.

The methodology part should explain how you want to answer your research questions (RQs) by explaining your methods and things like target population or practical realization of a method. It is sufficient if the reader can imagine how you want to answer your RQs and understand why you chose certain methods and how you want to use them roughly. Additionally the methods should be realistic for the scope of a bachelor's thesis. As in the part for the first RQ you mention limitations and possible problems and explain the methods you want to use in the part for the second RQ as well. Still I found three criticisms in that part that I want to expand on now. Firstly you often talk about models that you want to examine or expand on. Explaining what exactly is meant by model would make the methodology easier to understand as there are many types of models in AI. You could also explain that in the theoretical framework or SOTA. A question that I posed myself is for example whether these models are LLMs that are expanded with holographic technology and learned body/face movements. Knowing this would help the reader understand what you want to research. You could try to find literature about specific models in holographic AI and maybe even mention a specific example model that you will look at in the project.

Furthermore, you mention in your methodology part but also in the schedule in the weeks 17-18 that you want to propose a better solution if existing ones are insufficient. If you mean that you want to train a model yourself then I think this would be too much for just two weeks and could even be a topic for a whole bachelor's thesis. Explaining in greater detail what you mean by proposing a new model and restricting yourself on giving ideas that could improve existing models would keep the methodology realistic and clear.

Lastly you mention that you want to look at some less explored models. But elaborating whether a model is suitable for a use case requires testing that model and for less explored models there might not be much information on the model's performance in specific areas. Adding how you want to test them if not much information is given would add to understanding your methods and having a better idea of what you will be doing in the project. To find out how you want to test them you can look at papers that evaluate other models and try to understand their approaches and methods for evaluating these models. Then you could consider whether these approaches and methods are suitable for your case.

One small remark that is not really a criticism is that you mix up passive, the academic 'we' and 'I' a few times. An example is the part '[...] will be accomplished by mostly source research, while letting the need of the surveys flow into my work. Hereby, we will combine [...]'. Avoiding using 'I' in the exposé and using mostly the passive and the academic 'we' when needed would increase the formality of the exposé and make it easier readable.
