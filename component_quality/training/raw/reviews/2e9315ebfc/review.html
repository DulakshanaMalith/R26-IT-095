Weaknesses:

  • Minor weakness: Grammar and comma mistakes can hinder the understandability of an expose and distract from the main things. I found your lacks of commas quite difficult to read in some sentences and had to re-read several of them before understanding. Before submitting, you might let a friend proofread or a grammar checking tool detect such mistakes.
  • Science lives off precise and clear language and definitions. There were multiple occasions of unclear expressions or sentences (e.g. "those models", "specialized tasks like these", "How efficient is fine tuning..." without explaining what you mean by efficiency, what is "maximize [...] reasoning" etc.). Pay attention to detail and don't skip over unclarities just because it makes writing easier.
  • Science is also based on proper citations and references, to avoid plagiarism and to make sure what is your opinion and what is previous work. When mention "current work", "several studies" etc or when mentioning specific models like ChatGPT, LLama etc., you should always include correct references for a proper scientific work. When in doubt - cite :)
  • Precision and detail should also be given in the big picture to understand the expose's idea. I have many open questions (like where you plan to get training data from, what corpus you want to use for fine-tuning your data, what kind of model architecture and data you plan to use for the development of a new specialized language model, where do you get all the computing power from for the training of a new specialized language model etc). Decisions are important - but always include a WHY, and if your "main focus" is the development of a new model, you could provide some insights on your ideas.
  • The SOTA section should give an overview over relevant related work, present different approaches, their ideas and their limitations. In your case, I am missing all sorts of relevant work on a) finetuned models on healthcare (like MedPALM etc), and b) specialized language models in general (to understand your motivation better). Remember that the better and more complete the SOTA section is, the easier it is to justify your own research and new ideas based on existing work.

A good research question is very precise, well scoped, and "answerable". Your RQs lack precision and are very open, leading to your methodology - which would be quite solid - not answering your RQs fully. When scoping your RQs, consider whether you are actually able to answer them with a certain methodology or whether you should narrow it down further.
