The exposé begins with an impactful statistic “hospitals generate 50 petabytes of data per year, of which 97% is not analysed". This clearly defines the scope of the problem and highlights the potential for utilising such massive volumes of data. The problem statement identifies the critical gap in current medical procedures, especially the lack of integration of different patient data into personalised treatment programs and therefore makes it an excellent start for the hook.
Particularly in the context of cancer and chronic illnesses, the discussion between individualised and universal treatment is clearly articulated. However, some sentences such as “Large volumes of data that are available to create individualized treatment are ignored”, could be written efficiently for clarity. Additionally , by mentioning a small example of how personalised treatment may work better than generalised strategies would add more understanding to the study.
Overall the Motivation is framed excellently about the research challenge and the relevance of generative AI as a solution is made clear.

The research questions examines the study's practical and ethical dimensions and are well aligned with the domain of problem statement. They show challenges involved in integrating generative AI into healthcare.
1.For example the R.Q “What types of patient data (e.g., genetic information, lifestyle factors, medical history) are best suited for input into Generative AI models? This research question is precise and specific, focusing on the important task of determining relevant data types for AI training.
2.“What are the primary ethical challenges faced by Generative AI in processing patient data, and how can these challenges be addressed?” is a strong and significant question which could be further adjusted by the concern "what measures can be taken to effectively manage data privacy concerns in Generative AI?"
In all, the research questions are highly relevant to the case study and remain in the domain with a great clarity.

The exposé establishes its research within the theoretical framework of Evidence-Based Medicine (EBM), which prioritizes the customization of treatment approaches to meet the specific needs of patients. This provides a strong foundation,  as it is closely aligned with the objectives of personalized treatment strategies.
The mixed-method methodology, which integrates both qualitative and quantitative methodologies, aligns effectively with the research aims. A thorough examination of genomic datasets, including the 1000 Genomes Project, illustrates a comprehensive framework for selecting appropriate data for artificial intelligence models. The focus on integrating patient-specific genetic variations into treatment recommendations is particularly commendable.

The references are diverse and relevant, covering foundational theories, genomic datasets, and AI applications in healthcare.
