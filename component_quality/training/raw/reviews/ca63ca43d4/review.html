Review of Expose "From Data to Decisions: How Training Data Quality Shapes Generative AI-Driven Personalized Treatment Plans"

While the topic of the expose is relevant, it might not be specific enough, but the structure is clear and well-organized. Overall, the approach is comprehensible, but certain areas need further elaboration.

Summary

In this expose, it is stated that the usage of generative AI can help create personalized treatment plans, but there is still a lack of research regarding the quality and diversity of used training data and it's impact.

To analyze this topic, selected datasets will be evaluated and interviews with healthcare professionals will be conducted.

Strengths and Weaknesses

The hook should be used to introduce the reader to the topic and make them overthink.

It should specifically demonstrate what would happen if the generative AI produces false information.

To be successful, the hook needs to deliver something exciting or shocking while also introducing the reader into the topic.

It’s a good idea to mention that AI fails when too little data is used, but a more concrete example might be more effective.

This would engage the reader more, as it would feel more relevant to them. The general example lacks connection and makes it harder for the reader to visualize the consequences.

By using a real-world scenario, the reader could better understand the risks of AI errors and how they might impact their own decisions or experiences

To enhance your motivation, try focusing on creating examples that are more concrete and resonate with the reader.

It is a challenge to combine a fitting example with capturing the reader's interest, but it will strengthen your ability to engage the audience.

By presenting a relevant and compelling example, you will make the reader more attentive and increase the perceived relevance of your topic.

The theoretical framework is used to demonstrate theoretical concepts which are related to the exposes topic.

It should present the concept clearly and additionally show how exactly the generative AI might stand in relation with it.

To have a successful theoretical framework, it should be understandable and critical overlapping points should be defined clearly.

First of all, the concepts from Wang and Strong as well as Mehrabi et al. are both explained clearly.

But it is never explained how exactly these concepts are applied to the generative AI and how the identified problems will be addressed.

Additionally, it is mentioned, that the research will help advance in ethical dimension without providing clear examples for addressing these ethical concerns in practice.

While diversity and fairness are mentioned, the framework still doesn't really demonstrate a way to approach these concerns.

To further develop your theoretical skills, you should focus more on clearly linking theoretical concepts to generative AI.

You could create concrete examples that show how the data quality dimensions and the bias theory can be applied to AI models in practice.

By critically addressing ethical challenges, such as ensuring diversity in datasets and providing strategies for implementation, you can strengthen practical relevance of your theoretical framework.

This will help you conduct a deeper analysis and improve your understanding of the interaction between data quality, diversity, and generative AI application.

The first paragraph of the methodology aims to clearly present an approach that effectively addresses the stated research question.

This method should demonstrate the difference of using varying datasets and additionally fulfill all criteria needed for a well structured and complete methodology.

To be successful, the method should be specific enough for practical application.

It is good that you already have a selection of different dataset portals like GovData and FDZ. These resources provide a solid foundation for the analysis.

However, you only say that you want to conduct a "systematic evaluation of generative AI systems".

This seems unspecific and it isn't clear whether you are going to use existing models our develop your own prototype.

It would make sense for you to develop your own custom model and use the selected datasets as training data.

To further improve your methodology, you should focus on developing a clear plan for creating this custom model.

Consider breaking down your methodology into actionable steps, such as collecting and preprocessing data as well as designing and training the model.

After that, you can conduct performance evaluations under varying conditions of data quality and diversity.

By comparing these results and adjusting the model you will be able to improve your self regulation and fine tune different approaches.

All important parts within the motivation and approach are mentioned.

The SOTA refers to important papers, providing a solid understanding of the current research state, providing the reader with a solid baseline.

As mentioned both the theoretical framework and the methodology have a clear structure but some parts need further explanation.

Others

First of all, there still isn't a subtitle, which is important as it provides the reader with more detail about the topic.

In your motivation part the term "generated" is repeated quite often. Especially in the last paragraph of your motivation you mention "generative AI-generated personalized treatment plans" which worsens readability.

Maybe you could use alternative terms like create or produce.

Additionally, you could try to add a buffer in the middle of your schedule just to make sure you still have time as unexpected events and miscalculation can always happen.
