Summary

The exposé addresses overcrowded healthcare systems by using LLM-based chatbots to prioritize patient urgency in appointment scheduling. The goal is to reduce waiting times, improve patient satisfaction, and ease staff workload. The approach involves adapting a diagnostic chatbot with a K-Nearest Neighbour (KNN) model for urgency classification and testing its impact through surveys, interviews, and performance analysis at a GP practice.

Strengths

    • Relevance and Timeliness:
      • The exposé addresses a critical and highly relevant issue in healthcare: optimizing appointment scheduling to reduce patient wait times and improve healthcare staff efficiency. Given the ongoing strain on healthcare systems globally, this research has clear practical and societal implications.
    • Motivation and Context:
      • The problem is well-motivated with detailed statistics and references that underscore the urgency and significance of the research. The discussion of current challenges, such as long waiting times and overworked medical staff, creates a strong foundation for the proposed solution.
    • Clear Research Questions:
      • The primary and sub-research questions are well-defined, addressing both technical and social aspects of chatbot integration in healthcare. This demonstrates a holistic approach to the problem.
    • Comprehensive Methodology:
      • The methodology is thorough and combines technical implementation, performance testing, and user satisfaction surveys, ensuring a robust evaluation of the proposed solution. The use of queuing theory and a mixed-methods approach enhances the rigor of the research.
    • Feasibility and Planning:
      • The exposé outlines a clear and detailed timeline with specific milestones, indicating careful planning and feasibility. The inclusion of buffer time is a practical touch that acknowledges potential challenges.

Weaknesses and Areas for Improvement

    • Limited Discussion of Ethical Considerations:
      • While trust in AI is mentioned, the exposé could delve deeper into ethical concerns such as data privacy, bias in AI models, and the implications of misclassification of urgency.
    • Trust and Patient Adoption:
      • The document briefly mentions trust but does not provide a robust framework for measuring or enhancing it. Including a specific plan to build patient trust (e.g., user-friendly design, transparency) would be valuable.

Conclusion

This exposé demonstrates strong potential. By addressing the weaknesses, this exposé could be even better. The proposed research is both timely and impactful, and I look forward to seeing the final version.
