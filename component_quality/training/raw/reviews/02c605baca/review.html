Summary

The goal of this work is to explore the potential of holographic Artificial Intelligence (AI) partners in building emotional bonds with humans, focusing on their impact on social norms and interpersonal relationships. It examines both the opportunities and risks of these systems, addressing technical, social, and ethical aspects but also factors influencing user acceptance. The work combines qualitative interviews and quantitative online surveys, involving both potential users and experts, and focuses on perspectives from the fields of Artificial Intelligence (AI), Augmented Reality (AR), and Virtual Reality (VR).

Methodology (# methodology)

The goal of a methodology section is to provide methods that are tailored to answer all aspects of your research questions, while acknowledging and addressing possible difficulties and limitations. You should describe the research methodology in detail and use well-established methods and materials such as standard questionnaires. In this context the goal of finding suitable methods for your Exposé was to give you insights into scientific methods used in the research area of your topic, contributing to gain self-assurance in scientific work.

The method of qualitative interviews is well described with type of interview, potential participants and the aims of the interviews.

You have introduced qualitative interviews and quantitative online surveys as your primary research method. By specifying the fields of which experts are included as participants and by mentioning that potential users are also involved, you ensured that both perspectives are taken into account. By using MAXQDA for identifying recurring patterns and themes and SPSS to find statistically significant correlations, you have chosen well-known, well researched software for qualitative and quantitative data analysis.

You have established strong methods for your work, but you may be able to improve your methodology at some points:

  • You may want to clarify how you contact participants. Especially using social networks may introduce ethical risks that you should consider (e.g., by using micro-targeting). An option to reduce these risks is to specify your social media usage as a public post that invites everybody to register as a potential participant.
  • It is unclear how exactly you assess technical knowledge and social attitudes and how you use them in evaluation. You should clarify whether you use a likert-scale questionnaire, a quiz, a social experiment, shock therapy, etc. In order to be able to assess technical knowledge and especially social attitudes, you should define or cite theoretical frameworks against which these properties can be evaluated.
  • It is unclear what the online survey asks besides age, technical knowledge and social attitudes. Please go more into detail about which aspects of user acceptance towards AI partners are assessed. Maybe you can find a standard questionnaire on the acceptance of AI systems and on holographic user interfaces or create a new one, in order to identify relevant aspects that should be assessed.
  • When using SPSS only for evaluating the survey, you should clarify that it is assessing quantitative data only, e.g., by specifying the online survey as a questionnaire with likert-scale questions only.

Inspecting other research in the same field helps to reflect on your methodology by comparing methods established scientists have used for e.g., acquiring participants and designing online surveys, with your own approaches. Reviewing literature about how other researchers assessed certain properties will help you to find suitable methods to assess these properties yourself. This enables you to clearly describe how exactly you design certain methods, what you plan to assess with them and how this answers your research question.

Weaknesses

Research Questions (#rq)

A goal when writing an Exposé is to define precise research questions that clearly reflect which aspects of the research problem you identified during literature review, should be examined by your methodology. Research questions must allow for complex answers to your research problem, while being focused on examining specific aspects of it.

I have identified the following research questions in your work:

  • Main RQ: Will holographic AI partners reshape our social values, norms and interpersonal relationships?
  • Sub-RQ1: Are these technologies even capable of building emotional bonds and the kind of human relationships we are used to?
  • Sub-RQ2: What are the potential dangers that they may pose in terms of disrupting social structures and human interactions?

I identified several weaknesses regarding your research questions:

  • The work you planned is much too broad for the scope of a bachelor thesis as it essentially plans to explore the whole field of AI partner implications, instead of just covering a small set of specific aspects of it. You should focus on singular aspects that are manageable to research within a bachelor thesis, e.g., user acceptance on holographic AI partners.
  • As long-term effects are hard to estimate, the data is limited, and the time frame for your bachelor thesis is narrow, your main RQ is not suited according to lecture 7. You could refine your research question by clearly stating that your research is about predictions of experts in the field and not about what will actually happen. But as technology predictions are often highly subject to the motivation towards the technology, you may want to completely shift focus of your research to another topic. You have mentioned in your methodology section that you want to shed light on the relevance and acceptance of holographic AI partners. This may be a more suited topic to focus on, as it opens up research questions that are manageable in the scope of a bachelor thesis.
  • Your Sub-RQ1 leads to a yes/no answer. You should rather use wording that allows for complex answers e.g., "To which extent are these technologies capable of building emotional bonds?".
  • You embedded your research questions directly within the text. Highlighting them as RQ, Sub-RQ1 and Sub-RQ2 would make them more visible and therefore easier to comprehend.
  • Stating that the "methodology is tailored to answer the research questions" is not sufficient, as you do not refer to your research questions in the methodology. Please describe how the methodology is answering the research questions by referring to them and precisely describing the methods you use to answer certain aspects of them.
  • You state that you want to test hypotheses on user acceptance, which are both not further defined and not part of the research questions you described. If you like to test hypotheses on user acceptance you should clearly state which hypothesis you are interested in within your research questions e.g., "How accepted are emotional bonds to holographic AIs among people that identify as Gamers".
  • You stated at the end of your methodology that, "The literature used serves both as a basis for formulating the research questions [...]". You already formulated research questions in your Motivation chapter, so you don't have to mention formulating them in the methodology as you should not reformulate them, but answer them through it.

I would like you to elaborate specific aspects of the research gaps you identified when researching and reviewing literature for the Exposé. You should think about which aspects can be researched within the scope of a bachelor thesis. If you are unsure about the scope of the work, you can always ask students that already wrote their thesis, supervisors or [REMOVED]. When formulating research questions make sure that they allow for complex answers, while staying in scope and focusing on specific aspects of the research topic. Scientific tone (#informal, #tenses)

The goal of writing this Exposé was to gain experience in scientific writing and research in preparation for your bachelor thesis. Writing an Exposé in particular should enable you to gain experience with creating a convincing topic motivation and a well-founded research approach, without having to persuade a supervisor. To accomplish the goal of gaining experience in scientific working, you have to understand the scientific workflow and be able to write with a formal, neutral scientific tone.

You can improve your work to better match the scientific tone that is common in papers and theses:

  • You may avoid using word combinations that can be replaced by a more precise word or phrase, such as replacing "and so" with "therefore". This aligns with the formal tone in scientific texts, reduces verbosity and improves conciseness. To find appropriate words to replace less formal terms, I recommend to use a thesaurus, e.g., thesaurus.com. Another way to explore more formal formulations is to ask a generative AI for rephrasing suggestions.
  • You used subjective magnifications, e.g., "the greatest". To match the scientific tone, remove these magnifications or support them with an appropriate source.
  • The phrase "has not been researched much" lacks formal tone as it is vague about what "much" is. Maybe use "Has not been extensively studied" or "has received limited attention in research" instead, as it does not use a comparative word and therefore is more neutral, which aligns with the scientific tone in research.
  • Avoid using the first person in order to align with a more formal scientific tone. Instead of writing "I" or "me" describe the process or use the plural forms "we" and "us".
  • You used future tense, e.g., "will be". In scientific work you should always try to use present tense and only use other tenses if present tense makes no sense. Using present tense often results in a shorter, more readable sentences. This improves reader engagement and matches the scientific tone.

In future scientific works, in particular your bachelor thesis, you should reflect if your work is written in a neutral scientific tone. By checking for and avoiding unsupported magnifications, usage of first person and tenses other than the present, you can improve your written scientific work. As writing in English with a scientific tone improves over time and with experience, you can try to train it by reading scientific papers and by writing short summaries about them. This helps to transfer commonly used phrasing into your word pool. By asking generative AI to highlight phrases that you could improve to better match the scientific tone, you get direct feedback that helps you to reflect on your common errors and evaluate the quality of your tone.
