Summary
The exposé explores the comparison between neural rendering and classical rendering techniques for creating photorealistic avatars. It focuses on metrics such as efficiency, realism, and adaptability to evaluate the potential of neural rendering in overcoming challenges like the uncanny valley effect. The research aims to provide a comprehensive perspective on when neural rendering outperforms classical approaches for rendering realistic AI avatars in real time.

Strengths
Relevance and Innovation:
The exposé tackles a timely and important issue in AI and computer graphics: the creation of natural and realistic avatars for AI assistants. By addressing the challenges posed by the uncanny valley and the demand for real-time rendering, the research aligns well with current advancements in AI and metaverse technologies.
Research Scope:
The focus on comparing neural rendering and classical rendering methods is well-defined. The metrics of efficiency, realism, and adaptability provide a structured framework for the evaluation.
Balanced Theoretical Framework:
The theoretical framework is robust, with a well-thought-out approach to evaluating rendering methods. The inclusion of the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) for realism is particularly apt for quantitatively assessing the visual fidelity of the rendered avatars.
Methodological Rigor:
The combination of literature review, practical implementation, benchmarking, and structured evaluation demonstrates a solid and comprehensive methodology. The plan to benchmark methods on TU Darmstadt’s Lichtenberg Cluster adds credibility to the proposed evaluation of efficiency.
Acknowledgment of Limitations:
The exposé recognizes the limitations of the proposed metrics and the challenges of making fair comparisons. This self-awareness strengthens the research’s credibility and demonstrates an understanding of the complexities involved.

Weaknesses
Research Questions:
The exposé should focus on a few specific, well-defined research questions, and describe experiments that tackle these questions in great detail. Although the goal of the exposé is stated in the motivation, it is not formalized in the form of research questions. In consequence, the scope and focus of the exposé remain unclear
Informal language:
 A scientific exposé should be written in formal, professional language. This exposé uses a quite informal tone and is written in first-person.  Sentences like „I will use my personal DSLR camera“ challenge the scientific credibility of the work. Using formal expressions like „We will use a DSLR camera…“ would improve the tone of this exposé.

Overall, this exposé covers a very interesting and cool research idea. By giving it a stronger research focus, it could be improved even more. I am looking forward to the final version!
