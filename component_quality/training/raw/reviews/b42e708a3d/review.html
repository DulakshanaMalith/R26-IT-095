1. Summary

The exposé “AI in Augmented Reality” examines the role of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in Augmented Reality (AR) and its potential to improve interactive and personalized user experiences. The aim is to optimize real-time object recognition, context-aware recommendations and intelligent feedback mechanisms through the integration of AI in AR. Challenges such as technical limitations, privacy concerns are also addressed. The research uses methodology including literature analysis, case studies and user testing to develop practical solutions.

2. Strength

  1. The exposé provides a comprehensive overview of the role of AI in augmented reality and covers both technical aspects such as object recognition and pose estimation as well as user orientation, e.g. personalized AR experiences. This shows a well-founded examination of the topic and its versatile areas of application.
  2. The planned case studies, user tests and analysis of existing applications create a strong foundation between theoretical approaches and their practical relevance. This methodology increases the credibility and feasibility of the research.
  3. The discussion of data protection emphasizes the relevance of the exposé and shows that possible risks of AI integration in AR systems are reflected. This makes the exposé credible, as risks are also addressed

3. Weaknesses

  1. Unclear focus of the research question: The research question of an exposé should be clearly defined and precisely formulated in order to make the direction of the work clear. There is not really one main research question, but all research questions are sub-research questions. It should be clearly recognizable whether the main research question is about technical innovations or user interactions. The exposé covers many aspects of AI in AR (e.g. object recognition, NLP, data protection), but the central question remains too broad. There is a lack of a clear delimitation that specifies the focus of the research. Create a precise main research question that is supported by the sub-research questions. Limit the research question by focusing on a specific core area. This creates a better objective and helps you to work better and more precisely with it.
  2. Lack of differentiation of research from existing literature: An exposé should explain how it differentiates itself from existing research and what gaps it attempts to fill. This creates a solid foundation and justifies the relevance of the work. The exposé mentions existing technologies and theoretical approaches, but there is no clear analysis of the specific gaps or weaknesses in these technologies. As a result, it remains unclear what the project contributes to research. What is the exact goal you want to achieve with the work? Add a critical review of the existing literature and highlight which specific problems the project addresses. What does your work want to show?
  3. Unclear target group of the research: An exposé should make it clear who will benefit from the results and how these target groups will be addressed. The exposé addresses several areas of application (e.g. retail, health, education), but remains vague as to which target group is the focus. Which group exactly do you want to address? Addressing everyone at the same time could be too broad, as this means you can't be specific. You should perhaps focus on one group. Define the target group more clearly, e.g. "The research is primarily aimed at developers of AR applications in education." This enables a more precise presentation of relevance and makes it easier to focus on specific needs.
