Feedback

Your exposé has a clear structure and includes key components such as motivation, research questions, theoretical framework, methodology, schedule, and bibliography. However, some critical areas require improvement to strengthen the overall quality and depth of your work.

Firstly, the research questions are not sufficient for a comprehensive study. With only one main research question (RQ1) and one sub-research question (Sub-RQ1.1), the scope of the study feels too narrow to address the complex challenges associated with implementing LLM-based teaching agents. Research of this scale would benefit from additional questions that explore dimensions such as scalability, cultural adaptability, or subject-specific effectiveness. For example, you could add questions that assess how LLMs perform in diverse educational settings or how these systems align with varying curricula. Expanding the research questions will allow you to address the topic in a more detailed and meaningful way.

Secondly, the use of an arXiv preprint as a source is a significant issue. While arXiv can provide useful preliminary insights, it is not a peer-reviewed platform and does not meet the academic rigor required for this exposé. This source should be replaced with a peer-reviewed publication to ensure your work aligns with accepted academic standards. Look for articles in reputable journals or conference proceedings that address similar topics.

In terms of motivation, while the problem is well-articulated, the argumentation could be enhanced with concrete examples or illustrations. Claims such as the potential for LLM-based agents to "revolutionize education" are compelling but lack supporting evidence. Providing real-world examples or referencing case studies where similar systems have been successful would make your argument more convincing and relatable.

The theoretical framework introduces relevant concepts, but it lacks specificity regarding which framework you are using and how it aligns with your methodology. A well-defined theoretical framework is crucial to provide a systematic foundation for your research. I recommend reviewing adaptive learning theories or other relevant frameworks and clearly integrating one that supports your approach. This alignment would not only strengthen the structure of your study but also improve the coherence between your theoretical and methodological sections.

The methodology is well-detailed and appropriate for addressing the research questions, with a combination of qualitative and quantitative approaches. However, the small sample size of 10 participants for interviews may limit the generalizability of your findings. Justifying this sample size or increasing it would address this concern. Additionally, while potential risks such as resistance from teachers or mistrust from parents are acknowledged, the mitigation strategies for these risks are not sufficiently detailed. Including specific steps to address these challenges would improve the robustness of your methodology.

Your schedule is realistic and outlines all major tasks and milestones, but it would benefit from contingency plans to address potential delays. This addition would make the timeline more flexible and practical.

Overall, while your exposé is well-structured and demonstrates a clear understanding of the topic, addressing the insufficiencies in research questions, replacing unapproved sources, and providing more concrete examples and a stronger theoretical foundation will significantly enhance the quality and rigor of your work.

  1. Expand the research questions to include dimensions such as scalability, subject-specific effectiveness, or cultural adaptability, and justify their inclusion.
  2. Replace the arXiv preprint with a peer-reviewed publication to meet academic standards.
  3. Specify the theoretical framework and ensure its alignment with your methodology.
  4. Provide concrete examples or case studies to support claims in the motivation section.
  5. Justify or increase the sample size in the methodology to improve the generalizability of findings.
