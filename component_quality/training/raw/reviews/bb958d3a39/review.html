Your exposé addresses a relevant and timely issue by focusing on prompt injection vulnerabilities in LLM-based grading systems; however, several areas require substantial improvement to enhance its academic rigor and comprehensiveness. The title, "Hacking the Teacher: Command Injection into Student Grading LLMs", is engaging and intriguing but lacks the formal tone expected of academic work. A more precise and formal alternative, such as "Analyzing Command Injection Vulnerabilities in LLM-Based Grading Systems", would align better with scholarly standards. The motivation section effectively introduces the problem but fails to provide sufficient depth or evidence to justify the research urgency. Statements such as "obvious problems that accompany the use of AI" are too vague and should be replaced with concrete examples or references to known vulnerabilities in AI systems. Additionally, while the exposé identifies LLM prompt injection as a critical issue, it does not explore the broader implications of these attacks, such as their ethical or operational impact, which could strengthen the motivation further. The research question and sub-question are overly narrow and insufficient for a study of this complexity. Expanding the scope to include questions on scalability, ethical implications, and specific types of vulnerabilities or countermeasures would add depth and ensure a more comprehensive exploration of the topic. The approach outlines the use of direct and indirect prompt injections but does not incorporate state-of-the-art methodologies or benchmarks, leaving the study disconnected from current advancements in the field. Moreover, the absence of a theoretical framework is a significant limitation, as it deprives the research of a systematic foundation for analysis and interpretation. Integrating an established IT security framework or adaptive systems theory would greatly enhance the study’s academic rigor. The treatment of countermeasures as a "bonus" undermines their importance, and this aspect should be repositioned as a core focus of the study, with a clear plan for identifying and evaluating preventative measures. The schedule is well-structured but suffers from poor formatting, as the table does not fit the page, and it appears to heavily rely on a provided template without sufficient adaptation to the research needs. Additionally, the schedule lacks contingency plans for unforeseen challenges, which undermines its feasibility. Finally, the bibliography includes several arXiv preprints, such as "Prompt Injection attack against LLM-integrated Applications" and "Signed-Prompt: A New Approach to Prevent Prompt Injection Attacks", which are not peer-reviewed and therefore do not meet academic standards. These should be replaced with credible, peer-reviewed sources to uphold the study’s integrity. Overall, while the exposé addresses an important topic, it needs broader research questions, integration of state-of-the-art methodologies, a clear theoretical framework, better attention to formatting and planning, and a more robust selection of references to meet the standards of academic research.
