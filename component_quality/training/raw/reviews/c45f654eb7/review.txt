Summary
The exposé argues that holographic AI assistants—a fusion of augmented-reality visualization and AI reasoning—could dramatically improve operational efficiency, decision-making, collaboration, and creativity in professional settings such as healthcare, education, and engineering. To explore this promise, the author frames one overarching research question and five sub-questions that probe training requirements, productivity gains, enabling technologies, adoption barriers, and creative potential. The study will use a source-based literature review guided by the Task-Technology Fit (TTF) framework to synthesize existing evidence. Anticipated outcomes include case studies, best-practice recommendations, and strategies for overcoming cost, training, and standardization hurdles, despite limitations tied to reliance on secondary data.

Strengths
The author uses a strong, data-driven hook (35 % HoloLens efficiency gain) that grabs the reader's attention.
The research questions are clearly stated in the motivation part, with different sub-questions


Weakness
The research questions are somewhat broad for a bachelor's thesis; they need to be further narrowed down.
Task-Technology Fit framework is mentioned but not explicitly linked to the RQs.
The target group is lacking. The authors need to further specify the target group of this research.
The methodology is too vague. It needs more elaboration.
The author should describe how  the research questions are solved by the proposed method in the methodology section
