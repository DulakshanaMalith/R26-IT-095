The expose provides a solid foundation by identifying a critical need to overcome the limitations of two-dimensional AR systems in high-stakes environments such as surgery, where depth perception and real-time interaction are vital. The motivation is effectively articulated, highlighting how holographic assistants can bridge these gaps by enhancing spacial awareness, emotional engagement and immersive interaction. These goals are part of the broader framework of improving performance and reducing cognitive load in demanding tasks. However, the research question could be formulated more precisely in order to establish a clear objective, for example by evaluating specific parameters such as improvements in response times, task efficiency or error rates when using holographic assistants compared with 2D AR systems. Defining measurable success criteria will ensure that the objectives of the study are clear and achievable, providing a concrete way to evaluate the impact of holographic assistants.

The integration of embodied cognition and multimodal interaction theories is a strength of the presentation, as these frameworks support the hypothesis that 3D representations and multimodal inputs enhance user experience. However, the link between these theories and evaluation measures could be clarified in order to demonstrate how theoretical concepts directly influence practical outcomes, such as user efficacy and cognitive load reduction. The mixed-methods approach described in the methodology is a well choice, as it balances qualitative insights from expert interviews with quantitative measures of cognitive load, response times and task performance. Nevertheless, the methodology requires additional detail to enhance its rigour. For example, the sample size of 30 participants, equally divided  between experimental and control groups, may not adequately reflect variability, particularly if participants come from different professional backgrounds. In addition, it is not know how these participants will be matched for expertise and familiarity with AR systems, which could have a significant impact on the validity of results. Including this information would ensure that the experimental design is robust and that comparisons between holographic and 2D augmented reality interfaces are meaningful. 

To further enhance the study, the scope could be extended to other applications in education and engineering, as outlined in the motivation, to demonstrate the wider potential of holographic assistants. Addressing the technical challenges, such as the computational requirements and real-time rendering constraints of holographic displays, would also increase the  feasibility and practical impact of the proposal. In addition, incorporating iterative feedback from participants into the design of the holographic system will ensure a user-centred approach, improving usability and overall efficiency. This iterative process can help refine the system to better meet the needs of clinicians, engineers and educators, leading to actionable recommendations for future development and deployment. Similarly, it would be useful to specify how the tasks of the experimental and control groups will be standardised in order to ensure the comparability and consistency of the evaluation process. By refining the objectives, deepening the methodology and addressing the technical and practical challenges, this study can make a significant contribution to the uptake of holographic technologies, providing valuable insights for academia, industry and real-world applications.
