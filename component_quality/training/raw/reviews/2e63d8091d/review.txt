The exposé explores the use of generative AI for direct patient interaction in healthcare, aiming to evaluate its accuracy and effectiveness in generating treatment plans compared to human professionals. The study will use a mixed-methods approach to compare AI-generated and human-generated treatment plans, focusing on patient experience and expert evaluation. 

The text touches on the importance of AI in healthcare and the novelty of the approach, where patients communicate directly with the Model rather that with medical professionals. 

The research questions should reflect clear and specific objectives, align closely with the problem statement and be well-positioned against existing literature. 

However, the RQs in the exposé fail to address the noted lack of theoretical frameworks in other studies. Like the examples cited, this research appears to be another "evaluation study," and it remains unclear how it builds upon or differs from existing studies, particularly those focused on performance metrics. The wording in the first question ("How accurate does the AI perform?") is vague and could benefit from refinement. One of the sub-research questions "What are ethical considerations associated with deploying generative AI in clinical settings?" is only briefly addressed in the patient questionnaire, which may not be the most suitable avenue. These ethical concerns are better suited for discussion by specialists in computer science and medicine rather than patients. The exposé also lacks detail on the scenarios where the technology would be deployed.

Providing clarity on its unique contributions and in what conditions the technology will be deployed, wether the answers will be a help for the medical staff or be used by the patients without further inspection, would strengthen the exposé. To improve the RQs sections, either remove the mention of the gap in theoretical frameworks in the motivation or elaborate on how the study addresses it within the RQs. It would be better to use more precise performance metrics or benchmarks (e.g., diagnostic accuracy, comprehensiveness of treatment plans) in your first question. Furthermore, focusing on a specific area of healthcare, rather than the domain as a whole, would provide more depth and relevance. The potential consequences of errors in such a high-stakes domain should be explicitly addressed by field specialists, rethink how you address the sub-question about ethics in your methodology.  Lastly, discussing potential outcomes and contingencies in cases where the technology gives incorrect answers would strengthen the study’s practical and ethical considerations. In general the field of healthcare is huge and it's best to focus on a part of it rather than in general.

However, the methodology section lacks depth in explaining why specific methods were chosen.
