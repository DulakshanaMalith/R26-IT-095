This exposé explores the potential of holographic AI assistants and its impact on user experiences in augmented reality (AR) environments. It highlights the current limitations of traditional AI systems, such as screen-based or audio interactions, and the need for more immersive, multi-sensory experiences. The study is grounded in established theories like Social Presence Theory, Cognitive Load Theory, and Flow Theory. Using a mixed-methods approach, it aims to evaluate the cognitive, emotional, and behavioral impacts of holographic AI assistants, with the ultimate goal of advancing AR technology to create deeper user engagement.

The author of an exposé should be understood as someone who is an expert on the topic and that is something this paper succeded in. It reads itself as a paper that includes provides a lot of information on the topic. Specifically delving into the concepts of Social Presence Theory, Cognitive Load Theory, and Flow Theory proves that there has been research done on the topic. Furthermore the paragraph that details the current state of the research is decently informative and the footnotes help back up the statements made (2nd paragarph under 2 - Approach).

"introducing a physical, multi-sensory dimension to digital interaction, enabling a more profound sense of presence and immersion within AR environments.

Immersion, often defined as the user’s sense of presence and engagement within a virtual space [2], is a cornerstone of AR application success"

The second topic higlights the difference in holographic versus other AR AI and the third focuses on the improvments that can be made with holographic AI. This selection allows the reader to first start the paper with something they are familiar with (Google Home/Alexa) to then go deeper in the topic as they understand the mattertfurther.
