Generative AI is transforming healthcare through advancements in diagnostics, personalized medicine, 
and drug discovery. However, biases in training data pose significant challenges, particularly 
in under-representing minority populations and perpetuating disparities in healthcare outcomes. 
This research focuses on identifying the origins of biases in data collection and annotation, 
the role of demographic under-representation, and evaluating the effectiveness of current bias mitigation strategies, 
such as fairness-aware algorithms. Grounded in theories of algorithmic fairness and data governance, 
the study employs a mixed-methods approach, including quantitative analysis of healthcare datasets, 
qualitative case studies on annotation practices, and expert interviews. By addressing systemic issues in data pipelines, 
this work aims to develop ethical and equitable AI systems for healthcare applications, 
contributing practical solutions informed by robust theoretical and methodological frameworks.

The expose is overall well written, uses comprehensive language or explains 
terminology where necessary. The focus is clearly set on data collection and 
curation, where the root causes for the introduction of biases are believed to 
lie (see second paragraph on p. 1). This positions the research nicely in the gap found in current studies and 
other works, which are also cited correctly throughout the document. 
Research questions are mostly chosen appropriately for this goal. Especially 
the first question, however, may be too broadly phrased, which can cause issues 
with the overall manageability of the studies, and their results, that are to be conducted over the 
course of the research. To achieve a more manageable problem space, the categories 
of biases could be further narrowed down, and also the relevant domains of data 
sets; in this case the medical domain. This is already done or at least hinted at 
in the other research questions or in the approach section of the document. It would 
be beneficial to already clearly state this in the first research question, so that there 
are no misconceptions about the scope of the research -- for both the reader, and 
the research conducting party. To meaningfully compare the sort of biases discussed 
in the document from the medical sector with other domains, the planned research 
has to be carried out first, so that it can then be put in contrast to biases in 
more generalized contexts. Especially considering that investigating intersections 
of multiple biases, as proposed in the second paragraph of the approach section, 
is already quite an extensive task. Variable isolation is already quite difficult 
with this goal in mind, which has to be taken into account, to prevent introducing 
biases into the proposed research. Concrete steps on how to avoid introducing these own 
biases are missing from "4. Addressing Challenges" in the approach section.
With all this in mind, the title should also be more tangible; specifically mentioning 
data collection and curation makes sense, because that is what is referenced in 
more detail later on in the document.

Overall, the research proposal is mostly well-structured. The schedule may be too tight, 
depending on whether the research questions are narrowed down as described above.
