Summary:

  • The goal of this study is to develop an interactive AI teaching agent that ensures group collaboration with equal participation of all group members, and evaluating this agent in an experimental setting

Strengths:

  • You chose a unique, very specific and interesting topic based on new related work, and motivate it well
  • You made the research gap quite clear and showed the contrast between current and your work

Weaknesses:

  • When using new terms, they should be defined or explained briefly if important to understand the text. Several terms like "Community of Inquiry", the "Socratic Method" or "dynamic session control" were mentioned, but not explained. Try to re-check all of your used terms whether you provided a good enough explanation or not.
  • Scientific writing is based on a good citation style to distinguish between your and others' work. Unfortunately, you are missing citations/referenes in several places where they would have been necessary. When unsure, it is better to have "too many" than "too litte" references.
  • Related to this, scientific writing also means that one uses a certain factual writing style without exaggerations or strong personal claims. "Needs to be addressed as soon as possible", "key to redefine the role of AI in education", or "change the education industry in a big way" are a bit over the top for an expose. Unless you have good reason for it (for example after you are done with your thesis and you delivered actual groundbreaking work), keep your tone of writing a bit down.
  • When considering the big picture, an expose should revolve around your research questions, any related work helping to tackle these RQs and a proposed methodology to answer them fully which gives the overall expose structure. Your RQs are not very specific or detailed, and as such not suitable to be answered in a thesis. Also (partially due to that), your methods would not suffice to answer your RQs fully. Tailoring your methodology towards your RQs should go hand in hand with making your RQs more specific and narrowing down their scope.
  • The motivation section should already include what scientific domain you are in and, broadly, what current work exists. Your section on motivation is a quite informal introductionn to the topic from your point of view and lacks scientific context. If no "perfectly fitting" context exists, think of the different parts (AI teaching agents, group dynamics/psychology, group-based learning etc) since they are all relevant to the reader.

Others:

  • Even though it is totally ok to be creative with titles, I don't believe that your title actually reflects what your expose is about.
