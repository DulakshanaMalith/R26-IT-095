Summary:
The exposé explores the integration of holographic AI assistants in augmented reality (AR) to analyze their potential to reshape human interaction while addressing the social and ethical implications of their adoption. It highlights the transformative promise of holographic AI to enhance user engagement and emotional connection, while critically examining concerns such as dependency, emotional manipulation, and the erosion of genuine human relationships. The research employs a mixed-methods approach, combining quantitative experiments and qualitative interviews to assess trust, emotional engagement, and ethical considerations. Grounded in frameworks like Social Presence Theory and the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), the study seeks actionable insights for designing socially responsible and user-centered holographic AI systems.

Strengths:
The exposé addresses an important and current research problem by focusing on the combination of holography, AI, and human-computer interaction. It identifies a clear gap in existing studies, which do not fully explore how lifelike holographic AI affects trust, emotional engagement, and social norms. The relevance of the problem is well explained, showing how advances in holographic AI connect to broader societal and ethical discussions. The research questions are well-formed and align with the main goals, especially those focusing on user perceptions, ethical challenges, and the effects of relying on holographic AI on human relationships. The theoretical framework is strong, using Social Presence Theory to study trust and connection in virtual settings and TAM to analyze how users accept new technologies.
The methodology is clear and organized, using both quantitative methods like surveys and experiments, and qualitative methods such as interviews and case studies. This mixed-methods approach helps provide a complete understanding of how users interact with holographic AI, including differences across cultures. The exposé also recognizes potential problems, such as ethical risks during experiments and the need for advanced AR tools, showing good preparation. The schedule is detailed and  generally realistic, with tasks progressing logically from reviewing literature to collecting and analyzing data, and it includes extra time for unexpected delays. Ethical issues like privacy and emotional manipulation are addressed, showing a strong focus on conducting responsible research.

Weaknesses:
While the exposé is strong overall, some areas need improvement to make it clearer and more precise. The motivation and hook do not have a strong or engaging start, as there is no striking fact, statistic, or real-world example to grab attention and highlight the importance of the research. Additionally, the exposé does not fully connect its goals to wider debates in AR or AI ethics, missing a chance to show how the study fits into a larger academic or societal discussion. The State of the Art (SOTA) section identifies research gaps but lacks enough detail and critical analysis. For example, statements like "few studies examine the implications of holographic AI’s physical realism" are too vague and do not include clear examples or references to previous studies. Adding specific evidence and detailed examples from the literature would make this section stronger and more credible.
The methodology, although detailed, does not clearly define important variables like "trust" or "emotional engagement," which makes it hard to measure results effectively. These terms should be explained and connected to measurable outcomes to give the study a better focus. The methodology also mentions tools like Microsoft HoloLens but does not explain why these tools are relevant or how they will be used in the experiments. The ethical considerations are mentioned but are too general and lack specific solutions. For instance, concerns about privacy and emotional manipulation are noted, but no clear actions, like anonymizing data or ensuring informed consent, are proposed to address these issues.
Lastly, the timeline, while well-organized, is not completely realistic. Important tasks like participant recruitment and data collection are given too little time, which could affect the progress of the research.  Adjusting the schedule to allow more time for these phases and avoiding overlapping tasks would improve the feasibility of the project. Making these changes would, in my opinion , help improve the clarity, rigor, and feasibility of the research proposal.
