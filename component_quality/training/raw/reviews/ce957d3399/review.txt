Summary

This exposé proposes a Bachelor's thesis project exploring the integration of large language models (LLMs), specifically GPT-4, into introductory programming education. The focus is on using LLMs to automatically generate programming exercises aligned with Bloom's taxonomy, aiming to improve student engagement and learning outcomes. A split-study design involving two student groups—one using traditional materials and the other using LLM-enhanced content—is planned to evaluate the effectiveness of this approach. The exposé outlines the motivation, theoretical background, methodology, and timeline, using a LaTeX template and adhering to formal requirements.

Strengths

 Clearly identifies the importance of improving programming education and the potential role of LLMs.
The research question is well-defined, with supporting sub-questions that are concrete and aligned with educational goals.
The distinction between types of exercises (sRQ1) and student understanding (sRQ2) is logical and methodologically sound.
Weakness
 The introduction could better engage the reader with an attention-grabbing fact or question about current shortcomings in programming education
Some parts are repetitive or overly long—could be streamlined for conciseness and clarity.
The differentiation between prior LLM use in education (e.g., chatbots) and this project’s novel focus on exercise generation should be emphasized more explicitly.
Bloom’s taxonomy is referenced, but its direct application in the exercise creation process could be more clearly explained.
