Feedback on the Exposé: Hacking the Teacher. Exploring Prompt Injection Vulnerabilities in LLM-Based Student Grading Systems.

This exposé tackles a relevant and underexplored topic by investigating vulnerabilities in large language model (LLM)-based student grading systems. The motivation is clearly stated, emphasizing the importance of addressing prompt injection attacks as a critical pre-deployment consideration. It highlights the potential for students to manipulate grades through such attacks and proposes a study to evaluate both vulnerabilities and countermeasures. The problem is well-scoped, with research questions that focus on identifying existing systems, testing various attack strategies, and exploring methods to mitigate these threats. However, the connection between the stated goals and measurable outcomes could be clarified further. For instance, defining how the success rate of attacks or the effectiveness of countermeasures will be evaluated would strengthen the exposé.

The proposed approach is methodical, moving logically from scenario development and attack testing to countermeasure evaluation. The detailed timeline demonstrates a clear understanding of the work required, with tasks such as concept development, data collection, and qualitative analysis broken into manageable phases. A notable strength is the integration of both offensive strategies, like direct and indirect prompt injections, and defensive strategies, such as exploring signed prompts. However, the methodology requires significantly more detail to be fully effective. While the exposé mentions “quantitative experiments,” it does not specify the metrics that will be used to assess attack success. For example, will the study measure the frequency of successful manipulations, the extent of grading alterations, or the detectability of attacks by human reviewers?

The literature review provides a solid foundation, citing key works on prompt injection attacks and LLM-based grading systems. However, expanding the discussion to include broader security considerations in LLM-integrated systems, such as adversarial inputs or data poisoning, could provide a more comprehensive context. Additionally, while the focus on grading systems is valid, discussing whether the findings might generalize to other LLM applications, such as essay scoring or content moderation, would enhance the research's relevance.

Another area for improvement lies in the evaluation of countermeasures. While the exposé briefly mentions testing methods like signed prompts, it does not detail how their effectiveness will be benchmarked. A systematic approach to comparing these measures against identified vulnerabilities is essential to provide actionable recommendations. The exposé could also benefit from addressing ethical considerations. Given that the research involves creating and testing attack scenarios, it is important to ensure that these methods are applied responsibly, with safeguards to prevent misuse.

Looking ahead, the exposé would benefit from refining its success criteria. Clearly defining what constitutes a “successful” attack or an “effective” countermeasure would provide a stronger foundation for evaluation. Additionally, expanding the scope to include usability testing with human participants, particularly for the analysis of skimming-resistant attacks, would add depth to the study. Ensuring that these experiments adhere to ethical research standards is critical. Finally, discussing how the proposed methods and findings might apply to other LLM-integrated systems beyond grading would broaden the research's impact and practical value.

In terms of presentation, the exposé is well-structured and logically organized. The language is generally clear, but I have noted minor grammatical issues in the inline comments. The tone is professional, objective, and appropriate for the academic context. Overall, the exposé addresses a critical and timely issue, demonstrating significant potential. By refining its methodology, success criteria, and countermeasure evaluation, it can deliver impactful insights into the vulnerabilities and defenses of LLM-based systems.
