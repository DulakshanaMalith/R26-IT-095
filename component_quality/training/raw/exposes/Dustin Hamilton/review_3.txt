The exposé presents a well-structured and insightful exploration of the role of Large Language Models (LLMs) in education, effectively highlighting their potential to enhance students' critical thinking and problem-solving skills. A key strength of the study lies in its clear research focus, particularly the emphasis on practical applications of LLMs and their ability to support individualized learning paths.

The schedule is particularly well-developed, clearly outlining key tasks such as the literature review, theoretical framework, interviews, and experiments, along with a realistic timeline for their execution. This level of detail enhances both transparency and feasibility, ensuring that each phase of the project is clearly defined and manageable. Furthermore, the bibliography reflects a solid theoretical foundation by drawing on relevant studies.

The combination of qualitative and quantitative methods further strengthens the study by ensuring a comprehensive analysis from multiple perspectives. The inclusion of ethical considerations, such as algorithmic bias and the potential for uncritical adoption, reflects a thoughtful and responsible approach to the topic. Moreover, the aim to provide concrete recommendations for didactic design ensures that the research has practical implications beyond theoretical contributions.

Areas for improvement:

1/ Participant Recruitment Strategy:
Right now, the plan focuses on interviewing teachers who already know about LLMs (see Methodology). This might create a selection bias, which means the results may not apply to all teachers in different settings. To make the study more useful for a wider group, it would be good to include teachers with different levels of knowledge about AI, including those who are unsure or not very familiar with it.

To get a better mix of teachers, consider using a method that ensures different experience levels are included. This could mean reaching out to teaching groups or online communities about AI in education to find a more varied group of teachers.

2/ Binary Comparison Approach:
The proposal appears to adopt a binary comparison between LLM-based and traditional teaching methods (see Methodology). While this comparison is valuable, it may oversimplify the complexities of classroom dynamics, where hybrid or blended learning approaches are commonly employed. A more nuanced methodology that includes hybrid models or variations within experimental groups could provide deeper insights into how LLMs complement existing pedagogical strategies.

Rather than limiting the study to a binary approach, it may be beneficial to introduce a mixed-method approach, comparing different levels of LLM integration (e.g., full, partial, and no integration). Reviewing existing case studies on blended learning models could help in designing a more flexible and representative research framework.


Conclusion:

The proposal provides a strong foundation for investigating the role of AI in education. By exploring hybrid instructional models, and broadening the participant pool, the research can achieve both theoretical rigor and practical applicability. The well-organized schedule and thoughtfully chosen references provide a solid backbone for the project, and implementing these final recommendations will further enhance its impact and effectiveness.
