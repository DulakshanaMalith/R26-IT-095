The exposé claims, “Because synthetic data is not collected or measured like real data, it can be of much higher quality”. However, the author does not provide a source for this claim. As such, it is also highly flawed as this would suggest using synthetic data would always result in higher quality models, which it does not. Further, synthetic data is based on real, measured data. By design, synthetic data will contain errors, like measurement imprecision. Here, the author needs to be very careful with the claims.

Also, the paper states: “without regulatory hurdles or data protection delays”. Again, the work falls into the pitfall of not providing sources. In the community of synthetic data generation, it is highly debatable if creating look-alikes of celebrities, for instance, is ethical and even allowed. The paper lacks a reflection of the negative aspects of this approach.

Also, the claim that this approach provides an “infinite amount of unrestricted data”. Generative models and synthetic data generations can only generate stuff based on what they have seen in the past, resulting in pseudo-infinite states.

The Motivation further lacks a Research Question and a narrow explanation of what the work will tackle.

The Approach just states a bunch of theoretical background and fails to separate its own work. Phrases like “this work” are frequently used and misleading since it is imprecise. The reader does not know if the author speaks about a related work or their own.

The critique of their own approach is superficial and includes things like experts being hard to find and being very busy. Therefore, an unsuitable approach to conducting a user study is being proposed which will not answer the goal of the work.

Other things:

  • The timetable contains the definition of the RQs, which should already be clear.
  • The work states Author et al. in the Bibliography and misses meta data for the publications
