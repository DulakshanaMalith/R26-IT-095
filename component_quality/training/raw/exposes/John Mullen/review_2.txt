The study explores how large language models (LLMs) like ChatGPT can assist primary school students 
in constructively learning from their mistakes, particularly in mathematics. 
It focuses on using LLMs to create individualized learning paths, providing explanations 
and tailored tasks to address knowledge gaps. The research questions examine how ChatGPT 
can effectively assist with maths tasks, the benefits and limitations for students and teachers, 
and how prompts can facilitate learning.
Theoretical frameworks such as constructivism, the TPACK model, and self-determination theory 
highlight how LLMs can enhance autonomy and engagement. However, 
integrating LLMs into primary education remains underexplored, and existing studies primarily focus on older students. 
The methodology involves instructing three primary school teachers in Darmstadt on ChatGPT use, with students using the tool 
to review and learn from maths errors. Surveys and teacher interviews will evaluate the tool’s effectiveness. 
The study aims to reveal how LLMs can foster active learning through error analysis, though comparisons with non-AI methods are beyond its scope.
