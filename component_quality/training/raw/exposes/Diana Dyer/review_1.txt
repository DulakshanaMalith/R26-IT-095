The exposé sets a good foundation with a engaging motivation, its clear research question and a logical schedule. The introduction shows the significance of the given topic and the schedule has a structured plan for addressing all mentioned challenges. However, there are some areas for improvement, especially in the methodology and its details.

The introduction begins with two relatable and provoking questions: "When did you last see a doctor?". This hook captures the reader's attention and established the relevance of the research. 
Also the research questions have a good alignment with the problem statement, focusing on the effectiveness of fine-tuning LLaMA-3.1, the minimum viable dataset and the usability of the treatment recommendations. These research questions are direct and without ambiguity.
The exposé acknowledges prior work on LLaMA-2 in medical question-answering while emphasizing its focus on patient interaction, not on supporting medical professionals. This highlights the novel idea of the research.

The methodology must ensure that datasets are well-defined and ethically sourced to help answering the research question.
Currently, the exposé does not specify the type of muscle injury targeted or the datasets' sources and quality. Ethical considerations, such as data privacy and licensing, are not mentioned. This makes the project difficult to monitor and evaluate.
Clearly define the specific types of muscle injuries and specify how you select the dataset. Include information if you use a public or proprietary dataset and address ethical concerns, such as anonymization and licensing.

A well-defined fine-tuning process ensures that progress can be tracked and its results evaluated in benchmarks.
The concept of "incremental fine-tuning" is mentioned but not explained. There is not evaluation metric for tracking the models performance and adherence to medical guidelines mentioned. This limits the consistency in your methodology.
Explain how fine-tuning will be conducted in which stage and what data subset will be used. Define evaluation metrics and how the results will be compared against medical guidelines. Also introduce milestones to fine grain the iterative process.
Using established guidelines helps to ensure relevance and reliability of treatment plans.
The exposé does not specify the medical guidelines to be used or whether they are universal (for the whole world) or specific to a region. It does not outline if adherence will be measured or validated by experts.
Define the source of the guidelines (e.g., WHO, sports medicine organizers, Robert-Koch-Institute) and whether they are global or localized. Discuss the role of medical professionals in validation of the outputs and explain criteria for adherence.

The emphasis on making treatment plans  which are understandable and actionable for a general audience is a critical and often overlooked aspect of similar studies. This focus i a clear strength of the exposé and aligns with the broader goal of improving accessibility to medical care.

A well-designed survey ensures the usability of the model can be evaluated effectively. The exposé lacks detail on how participants will be recruited, the target number of participants, or any specific survey question. There is not mention of pilot testing or controlling for biases such as age or language proficiency.
Outline a recruitment strategy and a target sample size. Also include a pilot testing plan for the survey and give a broad definition for the questions. Use a standardized scoring system (e.g., Likert scales) to evaluate the outputs understandability and actionability.
Furthermore establishing benchmarks ensures that actionable outputs will be generated. 
The term "practically usable model" is vague and there is no benchmark or success criteria provided. This makes it unclear how the research's outcome will be measured.
Define what makes a model usable. You could use a minimum accuracy rate to achieve and adhering to medical guidelines in a way discussed earlier. Explain how the feedback provided by the survey will be incorporated.

Another clear strength is the choice of model. Selecting Meta's LLaMA-3.1 is justified by its availability and suitability for fine-tuning. Still it could be beneficial to discuss other models as well.

Coming to limitations and risks: Acknowledging potential limitations help to strengthen the research design and credibility. While the exposé mentions cancelling the survey if fine-tuning is delayed, it does not address other risks, such as data availability, ethical concerns or participant recruitment challenges. Discuss potential difficulties, including data accessibility and ethical issues, outline backup plans for survey implementation and recruitment.
