Summary:

In your study, you point to the problem that LLMs push the solution to a

problem too early. This has no learning value for the students. Or it

could also tempt students to cheat. You want to investigate how effective

prompting is in avoiding this issue. And give students a good learning

experience.

To do this, you want to evaluate publicly available LLMs, namely GPT 4.0

and Gemini 1.5, with prompting strategies for the ability to limit output and

hide solutions.

As a first step, you will evaluate 10 different prompts and test the models for

their ability to provide students with learning steps. Resistance to

jailbreaking will also be tested.

In a second step, you will take these prompts and create a learning environment

for computer science students to solve predefined problems. There will also be

a control group using only textbooks to solve the same problems. All students

will be surveyed at the end.

Title:

Your title is appropriate and aligns with your focus on researching the

effectiveness of providing learning steps without revealing the solution too

early, using LLMs.

Hook/Anker/Teaser:

Your expose effectively begins by citing studies that demonstrate the relevance

of your research to students, noting that 50\% are already using LLMs for

learning. You highlight issues like cheating and teacher shortages, to state

your goal to address these challenges by using LLMs to create a learning

experience that discourages cheating and provides personalized learning. You

identify the issue with LLMs and their tendency to provide solutions too

quickly. This is an interesting question to try to answer. Additionally, you

mention that no prior evaluation was done on the systems ("No evaluation has

been done on the readiness with which these systems will tell students

solutions if asked directly and if this has a negative effect on learning.").

But this statement will be difficult to prove. So you might want to rephrase

this sentence to something like "There is almost no prior evaluation of how

willingly, these systems provide solutions when asked directly and whether this

has a negative impact on learning.".

Research Questions:

Your research questions are well-chosen. They are a good foundation for your

study and align well with your title. However, the sub-questions are yes/no

questions. You might want to rephrase them as "How do students prefer LLMs that

do or do not provide solutions?" and "How do these systems enhance learning

compared to existing LLM assistants?" to provide a wider range in which you can

answer these questions.

SOTA/Theoretical Framework:

In your approach, you identify a researchable gap. That is feasible in the time

frame. Next, you explain what an LLMs is. Since your study heavily relies

on LLMs it might be beneficent to include some criticism on the

training on these models to inform the reader. They are trained on large

datasets that are not publicly available, and therefore it is not transparent

how a LLMs came to the output it generated.

In the next part, you mention that the quality of the responses of LLMs depend

on prompting techniques, and that you would like to explore specialty this

part. But you never mention the exact techniques you want to use. You also

mention these novel prompting techniques later in your methodology and do not

go into detail. You should clarify what you mean by that.

At the end of your framework, you mention two issues with LLMs and how you plan

to address them in your study. Namely, jailbreaking and hallucinations. I think

your strategies in this part are good.

Methodology:

Your methodology outlines using 10 different prompt to instruct LLMs in

providing good teaching steps to student. This will be using in the testing

phase. But you should clarify whether a single prompt will be used for all

experiments or if the best-performing prompt will be used. Also while you

mention groups of five, the exact roles or configurations of these groups are

unclear. It would help to specify whether each LLM will have a regular group

and a control group, as seems implied. Additionally, clarifying what you mean

by "variance for each tool" in the last sentence would also be beneficial.

Your chosen demographic, computer science students, could present challenges.

You plan to collaborate with teachers to assess students learning levels, but

university teachers often lack the detailed knowledge of individual students

learning level due to independent nature of university learning. A more

suitable demographic would be school students, or it would require extensive

research on the current learning level. Which could be difficult in this

limited time frame.

Finding participants without prior exposure to LLMs may also be difficult,

especially among computer science students, who are likely to have used these

tools before. Instead, consider including students with varying levels of

experience for a broader analysis

The sample size is small but manageable in the limited time frame. You also

mention that. However, you should outline a strategy for recruiting

participants and specify plans in case you are not able to recruit the required

number of students.

It is good that you address how outliers, hallucinations, and potential

jailbreaks will be handled.

Language:

The language of your expose is good, and I did not notice any grammatically

issues or errors

Schedule:

Your schedule is mostly well planned. However, you indent to prepare questions

about student's current knowledge levels before recruitment. This can lead to

overly general questions. And it would be more appropriate to reverse the order

to more effectively tailor the questions to the students. Since you plan to

incorporate the current student's knowledge base, with help of teachers, into

the questions.
