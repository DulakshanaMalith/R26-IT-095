Personalized Recovery in Action - Real Time Generative AI and Wereable Data for Post Surgical Rehabilitation in Total Knee Replacement Pacients.

Summary

This project addresses a significant medical issue within our society, identifying a notable gap in the field of rehabilitation, regarding the application of AI for its purposes. The research question needs to be refined, and several important concepts are missing from the theoretical framework. Additionally, the structure of the methodology is unclear and requires review and further detail.

Poject proposal

This project explores the potential of wearables and AI to enhance rehabilitation for patients who have undergone major surgical procedures. By developing an AI model that interprets data from wearable technologies and generates personalized exercise regimens, the project aims to assess its effectiveness in accelerating rehabilitation and reducing the risk of reinjury when tested with real patients.

Strengths

This is a highly relevant topic: individual characteristics in medicine, particularly in rehabilitation, are often overlooked. The research question provides a clear direction from the outset and is subsequently broken down into sub-questions. The state of the art (SOTA) clearly identifies the gap that this study aims to address.

Weacknesses

Research Question

The goal is to clearly define the scope of the investigation. It is crucial to establish specific boundaries to ensure that the research remains manageable. The sub-questions should directly contribute to addressing the main question. While the main question is well-formulated and clearly outlines what will be investigated, I have identified a contradiction: the title of the exposé focuses on recovery from knee surgery, yet the research question treats it merely as an example. It would enhance clarity if knee surgery were explicitly included in the research question, as this would provide a significant boundary for the study.
Additionally, the sub-questions need to be more focused, as they currently lack specificity. For instance, concentrating on a particular phase of recovery could be beneficial. The third sub-question may require an extensive study duration since recovery can take considerable time. Instead, it could be more effective to focus on a specific recovery phase or examine individuals' willingness to engage in individualized exercises.
